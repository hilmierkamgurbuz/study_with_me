So it assigns the obvious guess AND reports every renderer's submesh count, material slots and REAL UV bounds, labelling each as a page face (UV spans ~0..1 on both axes), edges (only part of the square) or tiling (spills outside 0-1) - the same diagnostic ChloeUsedRegionExporter.LogFaceUvBounds used to find the face atlas's true region. Writes the slot THE MODEL ITSELF names as pages and restores every other slot from the FBX's own material list. Slot 0 was the first guess and it was wrong: a page block is two submeshes - paper face and bound edge - and this model orders the paper SECOND, so the image landed on the edges. Reading the target off the source material names (fetched via PrefabUtility.GetCorrespondingObjectFromSource so it survives any number of runs over the instance) is the model telling us instead of us guessing - but a plain 'contains page' match then hit M_Book_PageEdge before M_Book_Pages_L and painted the SAME edge a second time, so PickPageSlot now rules out edge/trim/spine/cover outright and prefers the plural ('pages' matches M_Book_Pages_L, not M_Book_PageEdge), warning on a tie, and restoring the other slots from the same source is what lets a re-run REPAIR the earlier mistake rather than layer another on top. Refuses to assign at all if no source material is named like a page, since a second guess is worth less than a clear error. UV bounds are reported PER SUBMESH, not per mesh - measured whole-mesh, PageBlock_L showed one 0-1 span for two quite different submeshes. But the per-submesh numbers then DISPROVED the heuristic they were meant to serve: the edge spans U 0.003..0.997 / V 0.003..0.886 and the paper a clean 0..1, so both fill the square and the span cannot tell a reading face from an edge. The report therefore states what it measures ('spans the full square') and no longer concludes what it cannot ('a page face') - a full span only rules a submesh IN, a partial one rules it out. Materials land in Assets/Art/material as URP/Lit with smoothness 0.1 and metallic 0 - the 0.5 default puts a plastic sheen across the page under the room's spot lights - and an existing .mat is REUSED, never rebuilt, so hand-tuning survives a re-run. Page_Flip - the loose sheet caught mid-turn - is dressed too, and WHICH side's texture it gets is measured, not assumed: its bounds centre is compared against both page blocks' and the nearer one wins. Its material name says which surface the modeller reused, not where the sheet ended up in this pose, and the pose is what decides which block it hides. Skipping it is what made the right page read as blank while its texture had been on it correctly the whole time - the sheet was lying over it.
